Inside Tiger's Nest:
What To Know Before You Climb

12 March 2026 · By Sonam Tenzin

Every guest asks the same thing before they arrive: how hard is Tiger's Nest, really? The honest answer is that it's less about fitness and more about pacing — a steady, unhurried climb that rewards patience far more than speed. Here's what actually helps.

  1. 01

    The Hike Itself

    Paro Taktsang sits roughly 900 meters above the valley floor, and the trail up is about 5 kilometers one way. Most guests take 2 to 3 hours to reach the monastery, with a natural stopping point around the halfway mark — a cafeteria terrace with the first real view of the monastery clinging to its cliff face across the gorge. From there, the trail descends briefly before climbing again to the monastery gate itself, which surprises almost everyone who expected a straight climb the whole way.

  2. 02

    When To Start

    Leave early. Starting by 7 or 8 a.m. means cooler air on the climb, softer light for photographs, and a real chance of reaching the upper viewpoints before the trail fills with day-trip groups arriving from Paro town mid-morning. It also leaves a comfortable buffer if you want to sit with the view a while before starting back down.

  3. 03

    The Mule Option

    Mules can carry you as far as the cafeteria viewpoint — roughly the halfway point — but not beyond it; the final stretch, including the steep steps down into the gorge and back up to the monastery, is on foot for everyone. It's a genuinely useful option if you'd rather save your legs for that last push, not a way to avoid the climb entirely.

  4. 04

    Monastery Etiquette

    Cameras, phones and bags all stay at a locker station before the final approach — nothing is allowed inside the temple complex itself. Shoulders and knees should be covered, shoes come off before entering each shrine room, and your guide will know exactly which rooms are open on the day you visit, since a few rotate based on ongoing rituals. None of this is enforced unkindly; it's simply how the monastery has always been visited.

  5. 05

    What To Bring

    • Broken-in walking shoes with real grip — the steps near the end are steep and often damp
    • Water and a light layer — the valley floor and the cliffside can feel like different seasons
    • Modest clothing — shoulders and knees covered, ready before you reach the entry checkpoint
    • Small bills for the locker station, and nothing you'll mind leaving there for an hour

The moment the trail turns and the monastery finally appears whole across the gorge is, without fail, the part every guest remembers longest — worth every one of the switchbacks it takes to get there.
