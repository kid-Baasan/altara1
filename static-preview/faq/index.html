Planning Your Trip

Planning & Booking

Booking directly means your itinerary is designed by the people who actually run it on the ground in Bhutan — no markup, no middleman, and a planner who knows every guide and route personally.

Every journey is quoted individually based on season, pace, and accommodation, since Bhutan has no fixed daily package rate — on top of that, every visitor pays the government's Sustainable Development Fee directly. See our Tariff pagefor the current figures.

We only work in Bhutan, only plan private departures, and build every trip around how you want to feel — not a fixed template of sights to check off.

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) bring the clearest skies and the major tshechu festivals, though winter offers rare stillness and near-empty monasteries for those who prefer quiet. More detail on our About Bhutan page.

Getting In

Visa & Entry

Most nationalities do, and it's arranged for you through Altara once your trip is confirmed — there's no need to deal with an embassy yourself. Travelers from India, Bangladesh and the Maldives don't need a visa, only a permit.

A per-night fee paid directly to the Bhutanese government, funding free healthcare, education and conservation for its citizens. Full current rates are on our Tariff page.

Only two airlines land in Bhutan, both into Paro — one of the most technically demanding approaches in the world, flown only in daylight, good weather. We handle your flight arrangements as part of planning your itinerary.

Bhutan is consistently ranked among the safest countries in Asia for travelers, with very low crime rates. The main things to plan around are altitude and mountain roads, not personal safety.

Before You Pack

Health & Practical

Yes — comprehensive travel insurance covering medical evacuation is required for entry into Bhutan, and we can recommend providers suited to the altitudes and regions on your route.

Most of Bhutan's towns sit between 1,200m and 2,700m, which is comfortable for almost everyone. It only becomes a real consideration on higher treks like the Snowman or Jomolhari, and we pace those itineraries with acclimatization in mind.

Terms scale with how close to departure you cancel and are confirmed in writing before booking, so there are never surprises — ask your planner for the specifics of your itinerary.

Layers, whatever the season — mornings and evenings are cool even in summer. Modest dress is expected at dzongs and monasteries (covered shoulders and knees), and comfortable, broken-in walking shoes matter more than anything technical unless you're trekking.
